A Partnership Proposal for Kenya's Education-to-Employment Transition

Njia is a free, open career-pathway platform for Kenyan school leavers. It lists 502 courses at 150 institutions across 47 counties, with entry grades, fees and — unusually — a column saying where each fee figure came from.

This proposal sets out the case for partnership: what the platform does, the evidence behind it, what it deliberately refuses to claim, and where support would go.

What partnership looks like

Njia is free to every learner who uses it and intends to stay that way, so it is funded from the institutional side rather than the student side. Four routes, in the order they tend to fit:

  • County and institutional deployment. Kenya's National Policy Framework on Career Guidance (2023) names career information as one of five thematic areas needing resourcing, and most county education offices have no career-information system at all. Njia already holds one, per county, with the provision analysis to show where the gaps are.
  • Data licensing. The catalogue is published free as CSV and JSON and will remain so. What is licensable is the work around it: bespoke extracts, a re-verification cadence against a named cycle, and provenance auditing for organisations that need to cite the figures rather than browse them.
  • Programme and research funding. The county provision analysis is the finding most likely to change how money is allocated, and it is published in full rather than held back for a pitch.
  • Institution listings. An institution may pay to have its provision listed and kept current. It buys presence and accuracy, nothing else — see below.

What money cannot buy here

This has to be said on the page rather than in a policy document nobody opens, because the entire value of this catalogue is that its figures are not for sale:

  • Payment never affects ranking or ordering. No paid placement, no featured slot, no thumb on the Course Matcher.
  • Payment never affects a fee basis or an entry grade. A paid listing is held to exactly the same provenance standard as every other record: a fee is published only where someone read it off the institution's own schedule, and an unsourced figure is removed rather than dressed up.
  • A paid listing is disclosed as one, wherever it appears.
  • Njia remains independent and unaffiliated. If a listing cannot be honest, it is declined and the money with it.

Getting in touch

Open an enquiry on the issue tracker

Useful things to include: who you are, which of the four routes above is closest, and the county or institution involved. There is no form, no tracker and no mailing list — the same reason the app has none. Njia does not yet advertise an email address, because the project domain has no mail exchanger configured and anything sent to it would bounce silently — which is worse than no address at all. The tracker is public, is read, and needs nothing set up.

Corrections to the catalogue are welcome on the same tracker, and are the reason the data is published in full.

If you are a student, this is not the page you want

This document is written for funders and partners. If you are deciding what to study, these are for you instead:
