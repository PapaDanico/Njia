# Njia Career Exploration and Readiness Assessment (NCERA)
## Kenyan scientific research, psychometric validation and impact evaluation protocol

**Version:** 0.1, 15 November 2026  
**Status:** PROPOSED / NOT APPROVED / NOT VALIDATED  
**Audience:** research lead, Kenyan educational psychologists, career counsellors, psychometricians, implementation team, Claude Code reviewers, institutional ethics reviewers  
**Purpose:** specify a testable, auditable and Kenya-relevant evaluation programme. This is not an ethics approval, clinical instrument, standardised assessment or completed study.

### 1. Decision problem and theory of change

**Primary decision:** Does Njia's structured, evidence-backed career guidance improve learners' *career decision quality* compared with the career information/guidance they otherwise receive?

Inputs: accessible occupation and training information; learner interests and constraints; sourced programme/fee data; counsellor facilitation. Mechanisms: improved occupational knowledge, greater exploration, realistic pathway understanding, self-efficacy for decisions, reduced misconceptions. Proximal outcomes: career knowledge and decision preparedness; distal outcomes: applications consistent with stated preferences and verified eligibility, enrolment/persistence and employment/enterprise transitions. Contextual moderators: CBE/KCSE cohort, county, urban/rural access, schooling, household constraints, accessibility, connectivity and prior career exposure.

**Do not claim:** RIASEC predicts employment in Kenya, an interest match proves aptitude, a low grade indicates low potential, or a questionnaire predicts career success. Test those narrower claims separately if ever warranted.

### 2. Construct map and measurement blueprint

| Construct | Operational definition | Proposed method | Risks and checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| RIASEC-style interests | Enjoyment of representative work tasks across six environments | 6 subscales; initially 6–8 original items each; 5-point response + not familiar | Item familiarity, differential item functioning, social desirability, low exposure |
| Career decision self-efficacy | Confidence in researching, comparing, planning and asking for support | 12–18 original situational items; 4–5 subdomains | Self-report inflation; compare with observed tasks |
| Career adaptability | Concern, control, curiosity and confidence about future transitions | 12–16 original items; compare with appropriately licensed published scales where feasible | Cultural/linguistic equivalence; do not assume validated cutoffs |
| Occupational knowledge | Correct understanding of entry routes, actual tasks, professional regulation and costs | 12–20 sourced scenario items with explicit answer keys | Knowledge changes with intake year; update sources |
| Career values | Relative importance of service, stability, autonomy, creativity, income and work context | 12–18 preference items or best–worst choices | Values are preferences, not competence |
| Exploration behaviour | Number and quality of career information activities completed | Behavioural checklist and follow-up | Access disparities; do not reward privilege |
| Opportunity constraints | Grade/subjects, budget, travel, delivery mode, time, accessibility and support needs | Voluntary factual inputs; not psychometric scale | Missing ≠ ineligible; avoid discriminatory exclusions |

**Instrument separation:** vocational interests, self-efficacy, adaptability and knowledge are not one interchangeable score. Report each construct separately. Never collapse financial disadvantage into a personal-fit penalty.

### 3. Example original item bank (cognitive-interview candidates only)

**Interest prompts:** “I would enjoy testing why a machine stops working” (Realistic); “I would enjoy comparing evidence to solve an unfamiliar problem” (Investigative); “I would enjoy developing an original visual or written idea” (Artistic); “I would enjoy helping someone learn a new skill” (Social); “I would enjoy persuading a group to support a useful project” (Enterprising); “I would enjoy checking records for errors” (Conventional).

**Decision self-efficacy prompts:** “I can find the official entry requirements for a course I am considering”; “I can distinguish tuition fees from the full cost of attendance”; “I can explain two different qualification routes to the same occupation”; “I know how to ask a professional about their daily work.”

**Knowledge scenario example:** “A training institution is registered, but the website does not identify whether its named clinical programme is approved. What can you conclude?” Correct: institution registration alone does not establish programme approval. Include a source and date in the scored answer key.

All examples require expert review, cognitive interviewing, pilot item analysis and revision. Do not reuse proprietary scale wording without permission.

### 4. Kenya sampling design

Target populations: Grade 9 CBE learners and senior-school learners (subject to appropriate school and parental approvals); KCSE graduates exploring university/TVET; current TVET and university entrants; out-of-school youth; adult reskillers. Analyse these groups separately where measures are not equivalent.

Recruit across counties selected for regional diversity, rural/urban contexts, institution ownership, school type, socioeconomic access, gender and disability accessibility. Do not represent convenience sampling as nationally representative. Specify sampling frame, recruitment sites, inclusion/exclusion criteria, recruitment refusals and weighting before fieldwork.

**Illustrative stages, not power-derived sample sizes:** 30–50 cognitive interviews; 300–500 initial psychometric pilot; 1,000–1,500 independent validation; follow-up at 6, 12 and 24 months. Recalculate sample size with a statistician for factor structure, measurement invariance, cluster effects, attrition and expected treatment effects.

### 5. Validation analyses and explicit gates

**Content validity:** panel of Kenyan educational psychologists, school/TVET counsellors, career-development experts, teachers, employers, disability-access specialists and assessment scientists. Use structured item-relevance ratings and documented revisions.

**Response-process validity:** cognitive interviews, think-aloud sessions and observation of ambiguous occupational vocabulary; verify understanding of “not familiar” separately from dislike.

**Structure:** exploratory factor analysis on a development sample; independent confirmatory factor analysis; ordinal-item methods and robust estimators; inspect cross-loadings and residuals. Do not force six factors merely because RIASEC theory predicts six.

**Reliability:** ordinal omega with confidence intervals for intended scales, test–retest stability over a pre-specified interval, and precision at individual score levels. Cronbach's alpha alone is insufficient.

**Convergent/discriminant validity:** correlations with suitably licensed comparator measures and observed career-exploration tasks; pre-register hypotheses and expected directions. No claims from correlations alone.

**Fairness:** measurement invariance and differential item functioning across relevant cohorts and contextual groups when samples permit. Test differential missingness, accessibility, device type and digital literacy. If invariance fails, do not compare raw scores as if they mean the same thing.

**Criterion/impact:** assess observed pathway knowledge, source checking, realistic application choices and later enrolment/persistence. Track confounding and baseline differences. No “predictive validity” claim from retrospective convenience data.

**Decision gates:** no public normative percentiles until representative norming; no career exclusion by interest score; no employment probability claim without prospectively validated calibration; no individual diagnostic interpretation.

### 6. Evaluation design and outcomes

**Preferred pragmatic design:** cluster-randomised trial at school or guidance-centre level when ethically and logistically feasible; otherwise prospective matched comparison with pre-registered adjustment and sensitivity analysis. Both arms retain access to ordinary guidance; do not withhold required support.

**Primary outcome candidate:** independently scored career-decision quality rubric at 4–8 weeks, comprising (a) accurate occupational understanding, (b) verified entry route, (c) sourced affordability awareness, (d) alternative pathway identified and (e) a feasible next action. Blind scorers to allocation where possible. Pilot rubric inter-rater agreement before trial.

**Secondary outcomes:** occupational knowledge test, decision self-efficacy, completed exploration activities, realistic application intentions, application completion, course entry, persistence, satisfaction, harms and access equity. Specify each outcome's unit, measurement schedule and missing-data rules.

**Analysis plan:** intention-to-treat for randomised designs; cluster-robust or multilevel analysis; baseline-adjusted effect estimates with 95% confidence intervals; pre-specified subgroup analyses only; missing-data sensitivity analysis; multiplicity control for secondary outcomes. Publish null and adverse findings.

### 7. Career recommendation algorithm — research version

Keep four **separate outputs**: (1) interest/value alignment; (2) verified admission eligibility or unknown; (3) published known costs and unknown components; (4) occupational opportunity evidence and freshness. Provide explanation and alternative routes for each recommendation.

Pilot a simple transparent baseline before weights or machine learning. If weighted preference scores are tested, pre-register weights and compare with equal weighting and counsellor-ranked benchmarks. Do not present scores as probabilities. Never filter based on gender, ethnicity, religion, disability, or inferred income. Accessibility preferences must improve support information, not exclude pathways.

### 8. Kenya-specific evidence register and source roles

| Source family | Appropriate claim | Inappropriate inference |
|---|---|---|
| Ministry of Labour career-guidance policy | National career-guidance priorities and governance | Proof that Njia's instrument is validated |
| Ministry of Education / KICD | Current CBE pathway structure | Every programme's current entry rules |
| KUCCPS | Current published placement routes and programme records | All institutions participate or all intakes are open |
| CUE | University status and relevant programme approval information | All campuses/programmes automatically approved |
| TVETA | TVET institution and qualification/course directory leads | Current provider-specific fee or campus intake |
| KNQA | Qualification framework, recognition and progression context | Guaranteed transfer or employer acceptance |
| KNBS | Employment statistics and survey methodology | Individual job probability |
| KLMIS | Labour-market indicators and occupational classifications | Complete informal-sector coverage |
| Official institution fee schedules | Dated, scoped fee components | Unpublished accommodation or transport costs |
| Professional regulators | Occupation-specific licensing and scope | Universal eligibility from a generic degree |

**Verification requirements:** for each material claim, retain canonical URL, exact title, issuing body, publication date, date accessed, geographic/programme scope, observed claim, what it does *not* establish, and conflicts. Search snippets and inherited catalogue text are research leads, not direct source verification.

### 9. Ethics, data protection and safeguarding

Before collecting identifiable data: obtain applicable Kenyan institutional ethics approval and research permissions; determine whether NACOSTI research licensing is required for the specific study; assess obligations under the Kenya Data Protection Act and ODPC guidance with qualified local counsel. For minors, establish parental/guardian permission and age-appropriate assent as applicable, school permissions and safeguarding escalation. Participation is voluntary and separate from access to ordinary guidance.

**Production product:** local-only profiling by default, no account, no person-level tracking, explicit reset/export. **Research study:** if longitudinal follow-up requires contact details, maintain separate consent and an encrypted research contact store outside the production PWA; pseudonymise analysis data; publish retention, access, breach-response and withdrawal procedures. Never promise absolute anonymity when longitudinal linkage exists.

Offer non-digital participation and accessible formats. Do not ask for unnecessary sensitive attributes in the consumer product; optional equity-analysis variables in a separately consented study require minimisation and governance.

### 10. Research operations, reproducibility and governance

Pre-register hypotheses, primary outcome, design, exclusions, analysis and stopping criteria before outcome analysis. Version instruments, translations/adaptations if any, source snapshots, codebooks, scoring functions, missing-data handling and consent documents. Establish a steering group including independent Kenyan assessment expertise and learner/counsellor representation. Track adverse effects (discouragement, misleading exclusions, financial misinformation) and escalation routes.

Publish a non-identifying protocol and aggregate results. Do not publish raw minors' responses or personally identifiable information. Require independent review of claims before product release.

### 11. Delivery schedule (planning estimates, not commitments)

- **0–6 weeks:** literature evidence register, occupational taxonomy mapping, stakeholder panel, draft constructs, ethics scoping and instrument blueprint.
- **6–12 weeks:** item writing, cognitive interviews, revised forms, pilot software and accessibility testing.
- **3–6 months:** psychometric pilot and item revision, preliminary scoring report.
- **6–12 months:** independent validation and pragmatic impact study recruitment.
- **12–36 months:** longitudinal follow-up, replication and periodic norm/source updates.

Go/no-go criteria at each stage must be approved by the research lead. Do not claim national validation after the initial pilot.
