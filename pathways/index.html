Education and career pathways in Kenya

Two school routes are running side by side in Kenya at the moment, and they lead to the same places: artisan and craft training, diplomas, degrees and work. This page sets out how they fit together, and what Njia can and cannot tell you about each. It needs no account and tracks nothing.

The route most readers are on: KCSE

After KCSE, most places in universities and colleges are applied for through KUCCPS, the national placement service, using your mean grade and, for many courses, subject grades beside it. Njia's catalogue is built on this route. Every course card carries its entry grade, and the grade pages show what is open from C plain down to E. A learner with an E can still enter artisan training and some certificates, and the county pages show where.

The new route: junior school, senior school pathways and CBE

These points are as reported by the news outlets named under each. The official sources are the Ministry of Education, KICD (curriculum designs) and KUCCPS, and anything that matters for your decision should be checked there.

  • Junior school ends at Grade 9 with the Kenya Junior School Education Assessment (KJSEA). The first cohort sat it in 2025 and the Ministry said admission to senior school would begin on 12 January 2026. Reported by Education News.
  • Senior school has three pathways in most descriptions: STEM, Social Sciences, and Arts and Sports Science. Some outlets still describe a fourth, Languages; reports on the rationalised design say Languages was folded into Social Sciences. Reported by The Kenya Times.
  • Not every school offers every pathway or subject. Schools are described as triple-pathway or double-pathway, and subjects such as computer science and advanced laboratory sciences depend on a school's resources. Reported by The Kenya Times.
  • Placement into senior school, as the Ministry described it, took account of learner choice, KJSEA performance, psychometric test results, regional equity and school capacity. Reports differ on how the parts were weighted, so Njia does not quote weights. Reported by Kenyans.co.ke.
  • Universities. KUCCPS has said it will place the first CBE learners from 2029 using a pathway model, with competency descriptors (Exceeding, Meeting, Approaching and Below Expectation) in place of the KCSE mean grade. The criteria are not final, and KUCCPS has said placement will follow requirements set by universities, colleges and regulators. Reported by Radio Generation and Citizen Digital.
  • TVET. We found no published detail on how CBE learners will be placed into technical and vocational colleges.

Our reading, not a rule: a pathway sets the subjects you study, not the career you end up in, so keep your options broad until the placement criteria for your cohort are published. When you want to aim at a particular programme, look up its subject requirements now, because those are the part most likely to be written down in advance. Njia cannot yet tell a CBE learner which courses they will qualify for, and says so rather than guessing.

The ladder in Njia's catalogue

Qualifications in Kenya sit on a ladder. The Kenya National Qualifications Framework has ten levels, from basic education at Level 1 to the doctorate at Level 10, aligned to the East African Community framework and regulated by the Kenya National Qualifications Authority under the KNQF Act (KNQA). Technical and vocational colleges are described as awarding qualifications at roughly Levels 3 to 6. Njia does not publish a level-by-level table because the descriptor table could not be read from here; the KNQA classification standard has it.

A place is one course at one institution. Counted from Njia's catalogue on 13 November 2026. A low count describes what Njia has listed, not what exists, and a fee figure may be published by the institution or derived from a published rate; the open data says which for every course.

Moving up the ladder

Most ladders are climbed one rung at a time, and a lower grade does not close the route. Some institutions publish a second way in beside the grade. Njia's course notes record, for example, a bridging certificate at Carlile College for applicants who do not meet the entry grade for its diploma, and a route into a diploma at the Kenya Wildlife Service Training Institute for applicants with a D+ and three years of relevant work. These are published by individual institutions, so confirm them with the institution: there is no single national rule that Njia can quote.

Not every route starts in a classroom. The NYOTA youth project says it aims to certify existing artisan skills through recognition of prior learning, alongside business grants and on-the-job experience for unemployed young people aged 18 to 29 (World Bank). Njia lists it under funding, with its limits and how to apply.
